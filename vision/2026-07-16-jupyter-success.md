# What drove Jupyter's success

- Fully open source code
- Open collaboration with a minimum of private meetings/repos
- Composable and extensible architecture to meet diverse use cases and scientific domains
- Users able to get work done easily; minimal learning curve to using notebook
- The ability to combine prose, code, viz, audio/video, and interactivity into one document
- A Gallery of Interesting Notebooks, curated by the community, that demonstrated the domains served and capabilities of the notebook
- Initial focus was to serve scientific computing and education; data science came in the second wave
- The ability for the notebook to serve as a vehicle for sharing knowledge

## Some Jupyter Architecture Goals

- Meet people where they work
    - Terminal
    - Web
    - Local application
    - Sharing documents on web
- Focused on the core and trusted the community to extend where we didn't have knowledge or capacity
- Used the strengths of Python and JS working together
- Emphasis on "Don't Make Me Think" design in libraries, projects, and documentation
- A shareable artifact with a defined spec (`.ipynb` file)

## Gallery of Interesting Notebooks

The early days, [this wiki page on GitHub](https://github.com/jupyter/jupyter/wiki/A-gallery-of-interesting-Jupyter-Notebooks) was incredibly valuable. Curated by the community and the Project,
it shared the variety of domains that Notebooks were useful (sciences, social sciences, and humanities).
It also shared creative uses of the notebook and its interactive widgets.
